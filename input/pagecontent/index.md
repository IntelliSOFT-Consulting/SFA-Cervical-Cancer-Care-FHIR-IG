## Purpose
Cervical cancer remains a significant public health challenge, particularly in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), where access to resources available for prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of cancer is limited or nonexistent. More than 70% of all cancer deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries, and in Kenya, cervical cancer claims the lives of 9 women every day, with over 5,000 new cases diagnosed annually, being the leading cause of cancer-related deaths among women. The World Health Organisation (WHO) has consistently emphasised the importance of early detection and prevention in reducing the global burden of cervical cancer. However, despite these efforts, many health systems struggle with inadequate infrastructure, limited data accessibility, and insufficient integration of evidence-based guidelines into clinical practice. To address these challenges, innovative approaches are required that leverage emerging technologies to enhance cancer care, surveillance, and policy-making.

In response to these pressing needs, the "AI-Enabled Modelling of Cervical Cancer Registry Data for Enhanced Surveillance and Prevention Impact" project aims to harness the power of Artificial Intelligence (AI) to revolutionise how cervical cancer data is utilised across various levels of the health system. This is in line with the National Cancer Control Strategy 2023-2027 key area to implement a comprehensive monitoring, evaluation, accountability and learning framework, with mechanisms for data use, knowledge management and continuous quality improvement.
By integrating data from published scientific research and cancer registries, this project will develop a Large Language Model (LLM) with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) capabilities. This cutting-edge LLM-RAG system will serve as the backbone for multiple applications designed to improve cancer care delivery, public health monitoring, and research efforts thus linking policy and practice.

To align with this vision, this DAK will serve as a foundational guide for the digitisation and operationalisation of AI-driven cervical cancer surveillance and decision-support workflows, ensuring consistency, interoperability, and quality across the health system. It translates evidence-based clinical and public health guidelines into machine-readable formats that can be embedded into digital tools, including cancer registries, electronic medical records (EMRs), public health dashboards, and clinical decision support systems (CDSS). The DAK enables standardised data collection, structured decision logic, and AI-assisted insights at the point of care and policy, thereby empowering clinicians, policymakers, and public health officials with real-time, actionable intelligence to improve cervical cancer prevention, early detection, and treatment outcomes.

This Digital Adaptation Kit is part of the SMART guidelines. The contents are software-neutral, operational, and structured documentation intended to systematically and transparently inform the design of the AI system for AI-enabled modelling of Cervical Cancer Registry Data.
